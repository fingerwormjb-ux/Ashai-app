NSW Legal Rights & Protections

Clear, accessible explanations of statutory rights and legal procedures governing your case.

Right to Silence & Police Interviews Common Law / LEPRA

What it means: You are under no legal obligation to participate in a "voluntary" police interview or answer questions without your lawyer present[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span).

What to do: State clearly: "I am seeking legal advice and will not participate in an interview without my lawyer present."[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span)

Hospital Record Metadata Audit Trails HRIPA 2002 (NSW)

What it means: Electronic Medical Records (EMR) generate an unchangeable digital audit log whenever entries (gestational age, weight, or diagnoses) are edited[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span).

What to do: Instruct your lawyer to subpoena the EMR Audit Trail/Metadata from the hospital to verify the exact user ID, date, and timestamp of any alterations[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span).

Search Warrant Rules & Limits LEPRA 2002 (NSW)

What it means: Police can only search for items authorized by the issuing Magistrate and within specified timeframes[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span). Seizing unrelated paperwork can make evidence inadmissible[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span).

What to do: Subpoena the original Warrant Application and Police Execution Log to challenge improperly seized material under Section 138 of the Evidence Act 1995[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).

Subpoenas vs. Freedom of Info (GIPA) NSW Court Rules

What it means: Court-issued subpoenas are legally binding orders forcing agencies to produce complete logs directly to the court registry[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span).

What to do: Use court subpoenas rather than general access requests (GIPA), as police routinely refuse administrative requests during active prosecutions[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span).
