NSW Legal Rights & Protections

Clear, accessible guides on statutory rights, search warrants, and evidence preservation.

Right to Silence & Police Interviews LEPRA / Common Law

What it means: You are under no legal obligation to participate in a "voluntary" police interview or answer police questions without your lawyer present.

What to do: State clearly: "I am seeking legal advice and will not participate in an interview or answer questions without my lawyer present."

Hospital EMR Metadata Audit Trails HRIPA 2002 (NSW)

What it means: Electronic Medical Records (EMR) generate permanent digital audit logs whenever entries (such as gestational age or weight) are edited.

What to do: Instruct your legal representative to subpoena the hospital's EMR Audit Trail/Metadata to verify exact user IDs and timestamps of alterations.

Search Warrant Limits & Timeframes LEPRA 2002 (NSW)

What it means: Police can only search for items authorized by the issuing Magistrate and within specified time limits. Seizing out-of-scope files or unauthorized extensions can render evidence inadmissible.

What to do: Subpoena the original Warrant Application and Police Execution Log to challenge improperly seized evidence under s138 Evidence Act 1995.

Subpoenas vs. Freedom of Info (GIPA) NSW Court Rules

What it means: Court subpoenas legally compel agencies (police, hospitals, 000 dispatch) to produce complete logs directly to court registry. Administrative GIPA requests are often refused during active court cases.

What to do: Rely on court-issued subpoenas rather than general access applications to avoid administrative delays.
